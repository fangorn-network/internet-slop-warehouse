# Weird Worlds / catalog drop 02

Three self-contained 3D games, three reusable environment builders, six other logic modules and thirteen assets. All new records are CC0-1.0. The eight imported props retain their original Poly Haven creators and source links; original geometry is generated by `make-world-assets.js`. Three.js remains a separately licensed external dependency.

## The games

**Dead Letter Lake.** A momentum-driven electric skiff passes eight ordered lantern gates in a flooded grove. A 120-second route, boost, shallow-water penalties, a quiet unmanned post office, soft water ripples and drifting fireflies. WASD/arrows steer; Shift boosts. A route completion and timeout both end the run; Restart resets the clock and gates.

**Lunar Lost Property.** Haul a boombox, suitcase, watering can, chair, vase and ceremonial sword to a green depot under a ringed planet. E attaches or releases a magnetic spring. Smooth driving keeps the cargo close; the tether can break if stretched too far. No countdown. All six deliveries win the shift.

**The Quiet Engine.** A porcelain caretaker returns amber, tide and lilac crystals to matching pedestals in a circular sculpture garden. E picks up or sets down a crystal; a nearby matching pedestal gently catches it. The engine responds to occupancy with light, harmonic tones and rotating armillary rings. On restoration the garden stays playable, with no timer. Restart returns all three crystals and resets the engine.

All games support WASD/arrows, Shift, wheel zoom, Escape pause, restart, blur/visibility pause and touch controls. No local storage, accounts, pointer lock or external game state are required. Their artwork comes from procedural geometry and the warehouse's pinned GLBs.

## Reuse a world

`world-lantern-marsh`, `world-lunar-yard` and `world-resonance-garden` each export `attach(scene, opts)`. The returned handle provides `update(dt)`, `dispose()`, `root`, `spawn`, `bounds` and `obstacles`, plus theme-specific landmarks (`route`, `depot`, or `zones`). The garden also exposes `setPower(0..1)`. Use an environment without any of its demonstration game's rules, change the palette or seed, and add your own actors.

The worlds create their geometry and lighting locally. The skiff, vehicles and imported props are game dependencies, not hard requirements of the world builders.

## Other logic

| Part | Reuse |
| --- | --- |
| `inertial-drive` | Inject `{x,z,boost}` input; get momentum, braking, turning, box collision, bounds and impact hooks. |
| `checkpoint-course` | Ordered swept checkpoints, lap tracking, retry/reset and single completion. |
| `spring-tether` | Sub-stepped damped carrying or towing; inject a world-space target or null to release. |
| `trigger-zones` | Filtered circular occupancy, enter/leave edges and aggregate completion. No engine dependency. |
| `ambient-motes` | Seeded single-draw-call fireflies, dust or stars with explicit GPU cleanup. |
| `expedition-shell` | Shared WebGL renderer, image lighting, static/skinned model cloning and fitting, camera, menus, input, loading, synthesized audio and lifecycle. |

All modules follow the warehouse `attach(object, opts)` convention and label their exports, options and hooks for search. Time is in seconds. The shell owns rendering by default; pass `autoRender:false` to drive its returned `update(dt)` yourself. `build(context)` returns the game's `reset`, `update`, optional `ambient`, `snapshot` and `dispose` callbacks. `context.model(id, extent, 'max')` fits a prop by its longest dimension; the default fits by height. The read-only `worldDebug.snapshot` exposes progress for QA and remixes, not gameplay shortcuts.

## Assets and provenance

Original models: courier skiff, magnetic salvage tug, garden caretaker, resonator crystal and signal buoy. They use geometry and materials only, are small standalone GLBs and have forward direction +Z. Their generation recipe, hashes and mesh counts are in `originals/provenance.json`.

Imported props: boombox, vintage suitcase, metal watering can, plastic monobloc chair, ceramic vase, oil lamp, metal tool chest and antique katana. All are packed from Poly Haven's 1K glTF downloads into self-contained GLBs with byte/hash verification. License evidence, per-file download metadata and transformations are preserved in `ingestion/polyhaven/batches/weird-worlds/`. Powered by Poly Haven.

## Build, verify and publish

```sh
node ingestion/polyhaven/ingest.js --batch weird-worlds
node parts/make-world-assets.js
node publish-batch.js parts/weird-worlds.parts.json --local /tmp/weird-worlds
npm test
PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE=/tmp/warehouse-validation/node_modules/playwright-core/index.mjs node scripts/test-weird-worlds-browser.mjs /tmp/weird-worlds
NETWORK=baseSepolia node publish-batch.js parts/weird-worlds.parts.json
```

The manifest publishes assets and modules before the games, fills the games' `PARTS` maps with pinned storage URLs, and saves chain read-back receipts. Structural GLB validation reports and browser artifacts live in `artifacts/weird-worlds/`. Poly Haven models retain upstream missing-tangent warnings; the five original models validate with no warnings.

## Limits

These are compact single-player worlds, not a full physics engine or multiplayer simulation. Towing uses a damped spring, not rigid-body collision between cargo. Movement uses horizontal box obstacles and bounds. The lake is a route through open water; there is no submerged terrain or boat hydrodynamics. These choices keep the parts small, understandable and easy to replace.
